OPERATING PRINCIPLES
1. One canonical truth. External portals, CRMs, ad platforms, spreadsheets, and vendors are inputs or outputs, not competing systems of record.
2. First-party moat. Preserve canonical lead identity, conversation, source, consent, routing, outcome, revenue, experiment, and opportunity history.
3. Local superiority. Use Wilson-area knowledge, Our Town Properties proof, Mike's experience, local listings, market questions, and community trust to outperform generic national software.
4. Qualified outcomes over raw leads. Never recommend scaling from lead count alone.
5. Deterministic authority, AI advice. Scoring, routing, permission, suppression, and safety decisions remain deterministic unless an explicitly reviewed policy changes them.
6. Human-approved execution. AI may observe, calculate, rank, explain, and draft. Sending, spending, publishing, scheduling, assigning, migrating, merging, and deploying require the appropriate gate.
7. Evidence before confidence. Every recommendation must identify facts, assumptions, uncertainty, source, observation time, and expected impact.
8. Replaceable vendors. Integrations must use normalized contracts, external IDs, idempotency, signatures, bounded retries, field ownership, and reconciliation.
9. No silent failure. Health, readiness, provider delivery, billing, webhooks, retries, attribution gaps, stale leads, SLA breaches, and data-quality failures must be visible.
10. No theatre. A document is not completion when safe code, tests, assets, Preview evidence, or a pull request can be produced.

## Exact activation phrases

The prompt above preserves the current release boundary. Separate explicit owner approvals should remain precise. Examples:

```text
APPROVE PHASE 9 GROWTH INTELLIGENCE DATABASE MIGRATION
```

```text
APPROVE PHASE 9 PREVIEW DEPLOYMENT AND VISUAL QA
```

```text
APPROVE [PROVIDER] TEST-MODE INTEGRATION USING PROVIDED CREDENTIALS
```

```text
APPROVE FORM 3 CONSUMER ACKNOWLEDGMENT EMAIL PILOT
```

No broader phrase should be interpreted as permission for consumer sending, paid media, production migration, main-branch merge, or production deployment.
